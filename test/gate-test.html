What My Friend Already Knew and Somehow Missed

He had real faith — he just didn't know what to look for. A conversation in San Francisco about the perspective that changes how you see everything.

Mike Edwards  ·  Proxima  ·  Program Director March 24, 2026

We were somewhere in the middle of a long conversation when my new friend said something that stayed with me. He'd been following Jesus for years. He had good theology, a real relationship, genuine faith. But nobody had ever handed him a perspective for watching God work in ordinary life. He hadn't known that was something you could do.

I didn't grow up with that perspective either. Someone had to give it to me.

There's a moment in Acts where Paul arrives in Ephesus and meets a small group of believers who are sincere but incomplete. They know the beginning of the story. What they're missing is what came next — the Spirit, present and active, not as a doctrine to affirm but as a relationship to live inside. N.T. Wright reads it this way: Luke isn't just introducing Paul's work in Ephesus. He's showing that from the very start, Paul was concerned with the Spirit's actual work — in individual lives, and out into the wider community.

That gap — between knowing about something and having a perspective that makes it visible — is what my friend was sitting in.

He's not unusual. Most people I talk with in San Francisco who follow Jesus can name their faith clearly. What's harder to name is where Jesus shows up on a Tuesday. In a stranger's unexpected kindness. In the conversation that goes somewhere you didn't plan. In the thing that stirs in you before you've had time to think about it.

"He will guide you into all truth," Jesus says of the Spirit in John 16. Not explain. Not inform. Guide — present, directional, ongoing.

My friend had never been given that perspective. So he hadn't looked.

What shifted in our conversation wasn't a new piece of theology. It was closer to a new perspective. A way of seeing he hadn't been handed before. Once you know the kingdom of God is moving around you, ordinary moments stop being background noise. The conversation that goes somewhere unexpected. The pull toward a stranger. The thing that stirs in you before you've had time to explain it. These become something worth paying attention to.

I left without knowing if any of it landed. That's usually how it goes.

But I've been thinking about what it costs to go years with genuine faith and never have someone say: the kingdom of God is already moving around you. You're allowed to look for it.

What would change for you if that were the perspective you carried into tomorrow?

Perhaps not everything. But perhaps enough.
